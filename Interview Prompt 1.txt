THE SUBSTRATE

Copilot Concourse — your enterprise semantic substrate.
- A curated SharePoint reference library (The Farmhouse Concourse) indexed by Microsoft Graph. Holds canonical definitions, reference material, leadership doctrine, prompting playbook, and operating concepts. When Copilot reasons about your work, it draws from your authoritative material rather than generic web content.
- Why it matters first: every other instrument on this dashboard reasons better when the Concourse holds the relevant references. Garbage in, fluent garbage out — good substrate, good answers.
- Example: ask Copilot about OTIF, and it answers using *your* OTIF definition from the Concourse, not a generic supply-chain one.
- When: this is always running underneath. Your job as an operator: know it exists, know what's in it, and recognize when something your workflow needs *should* be in it but isn't.
- Constraint: it works because people publish to it. The Concourse is living infrastructure — there is always more that could go in.

THE CONNECTION LAYER

MCP (Model Context Protocol) — how Copilot reaches into your business systems.
- An open standard, like a universal adapter, that lets AI talk to systems through a consistent, governed interface. Defines what AI can read, what actions it can take, and how access is authorized. Before MCP, every AI integration was bespoke; with it, the wiring is standardized and reusable.
- Why "open standard" matters: Microsoft, Anthropic, and others publish MCP servers using the same protocol. Same wire format, same governance pattern, same audit shape — regardless of which model is on the other end.
- Microsoft has shipped MCP servers across your M365, Power Platform, and Dynamics stack — including D365 F&O, Fabric Data Agents, the Power BI semantic model, M365/Graph, and more arriving steadily. Your systems are already callable.
- Example: when an instrument below (Researcher, an agent, Power Automate + Copilot) reaches into D365 F&O to pull records, it goes through the F&O MCP server. You don't write any of that wiring — Microsoft has.
- When: this is the layer underneath the instruments you actually use. You don't reach for MCP directly; you reach for an instrument, and MCP is how it connects.

DAILY-USE INSTRUMENTS

Standard Copilot chat — your primary control surface.
- Ask Copilot a question, get an answer. Information lookup, drafting, brainstorming, summarizing.
- Example: "Summarize the key risks in this RFP."
- When: single, ad-hoc questions. The default starting point.
- Constraint: no persistence; doesn't run on a schedule.

Scheduled Prompts — set-and-forget recurring work.
- Native M365 Copilot feature. Write a prompt, run it once to verify, schedule it (daily / weekly / monthly). Results land in Copilot chat with optional email notification.
- Example: "Every Monday at 8am, summarize last week's vendor exception emails ranked by dollar value." Or: "Every weekday at 7am, brief me on inbox items from leadership and audit, flag anything time-sensitive."
- When: recurring summaries, monitoring, triage. The "morning briefing" or "Friday rollup" pattern.
- Constraint: per-user 10-prompt cap. License-gated. First use auto-creates a Power Platform environment.

Copilot in Word / Excel / PowerPoint — Agent Mode (in-app).
- Copilot operates in-place inside the document with full state awareness. Multi-step edits, model audits, deck restructure — without exporting and re-importing.
- Example: in Word, "tighten this 12-page draft to 6 pages, keep the SOX section, drop the appendix."
- When: document-centric work where Copilot needs to see and act across the whole document.
- Constraint: lives inside the app; not for cross-system work.

Facilitator agent — meeting-resident assistant.
- An agent you turn on during a Teams meeting. Helps keep the meeting structured, captures decisions, turns conversation into tasks and summaries.
- Example: turn it on for a steering committee — it captures action items and surfaces them at the end without anyone scribing.
- When: structured meetings where the work product (decisions, tasks, summary) matters as much as the conversation.
- Constraint: lives inside Teams meetings.

SPECIALIZED INSTRUMENTS

Researcher agent (GA).
- A "deep investigation" mode for Copilot. Pulls from web AND your work content (files, emails, meetings, chats you have access to). Produces a multi-step, source-cited report.
- Example: "Build me a brief on vaccine sourcing trends across regenerative agriculture peers, drawing from public reporting and our internal supplier strategy documents."
- When: formal briefs, decisions that need cited evidence, multi-source synthesis worth hours of manual research.
- Constraint: takes longer than standard chat. Combined 25-query-per-month per-user limit shared with Analyst.

Analyst agent (GA).
- A data analysis mode. Runs Python in a sandbox over your data (CSV, Excel, governed datasets), with chain-of-thought reasoning and visible code.
- Example: "Find any farms where Q3 vaccine compliance dropped more than 15% from Q2."
- When: one-off data questions, anomaly checks, trend exploration. You don't have to be a data analyst yourself.
- Constraint: shares the 25-query-per-month cap with Researcher. Operates inside the M365 commercial data processing boundary.

Copilot Studio agents — custom builds.
- A purpose-built AI worker with defined sources, tools, and rules. The full mechanism: a model + knowledge sources + callable functions + guardrails, packaged as a persistent worker with a job. This is "an agent" in the full technical sense.
- Example: a "vendor exception agent" that reads a shared mailbox, checks D365 for matching POs, drafts replies for routine cases, escalates anomalies.
- When: repeating, defined patterns where standard chat or Scheduled Prompts don't fit.
- Constraint: higher governance overhead — usually IT-built. (E7 adds formal agent identity and lifecycle.)

Power Automate + Copilot — event-driven orchestration.
- A trigger fires (file lands, email arrives, record changes), conditions evaluate, actions execute — including calling Copilot to interpret content or draft output. AI decides; Power Automate executes.
- Example: vendor invoice email lands → extract amount, vendor, due date → draft AP entry → route for approval.
- When: multi-step work needing approvals and audit trail.
- Constraint: requires Power Platform licensing posture; flow author must be defined.

Frame 2 — Where AI lands first: routing the operator toward the right pattern.

Looking at the dashboard, there are usually three shapes of work where AI lands first for someone new to it. Most workflows fit one of these — and knowing which shape you're in tells you which instrument to reach for.

**Pattern 1 — Recurring scan or summary.**
You do a similar scan, summary, or check at a regular cadence. Reading the inbox, scanning a channel, pulling a weekly status, watching for exceptions.
→ Scheduled Prompts territory. Sometimes Power Automate + Copilot if the work has multiple steps or routing.

**Pattern 2 — Deep one-off question.**
You need to investigate something hard. Synthesize a brief from many sources. Analyze data and find what's interesting. Build a position on something with cited evidence.
→ Researcher agent (multi-source briefs) or Analyst agent (data analysis) territory.

**Pattern 3 — Real workflow with multiple steps.**
You have a defined process that runs the same way each time. Records to check, decisions to make, drafts to produce, things to route. Today you do it by hand; you'd like AI to do the bulk of it within guardrails.
→ Copilot Studio agent or Power Automate + Copilot territory. Often the Concourse is part of this — the workflow needs canonical references that may or may not be published yet.

Routing probe: "Of those three — recurring scan, deep one-off, or multi-step workflow — which shape feels closest to where you'd start?"

After answer, transition: "Good. Let's find the specific work." Proceed to DISCOVERY TRACK.
